## Phase 1 -- Personal Prototype

### Core Problem

Thrifted paintings have unknown histories. There is no frictionless way to
identify a piece of art, retrieve its factual background, and capture personal
thoughts about it in one fluid experience.

### User Needs

1. **Physical trigger** -- Tapping a hidden, adhesive NFC tag behind a painting
   opens the app on a smartphone. The interaction must feel invisible and
   seamless (no visible hardware, no QR codes).

2. **Art identification** -- When no journal entry exists for a painting yet, the
   app prompts the user to take a photo. Image recognition software identifies
   the painting and returns structured information (title, artist, year,
   movement).

3. **Factual context retrieval** -- The system retrieves historical and art-world
   context from archival sources (primarily Wikipedia, possibly Artsy). This
   context must not be generated by a large language model. The information must
   come from established, citable sources.

4. **Guided journaling prompts** -- Based on the retrieved factual context, the
   system generates introspective questions to give the user a starting point for
   writing. These prompts are disposable -- the user can delete them and write
   freely.

5. **Fluid journaling canvas** -- The writing experience should be visually
   appealing and feel impermanent until the user decides to keep it. The
   interface should support block-based editing where factual context sits at
   the top, prompts appear as lightweight blocks below, and freeform text flows
   naturally underneath or in place of prompts.

6. **Frictionless ownership without traditional accounts** -- The user does not
   fill out sign-up forms, create passwords, or manage credentials. The device
   performing the initial setup (first tap) gains permanent owner/creator rights
   via a persistent, cryptographic token stored on the device.

7. **Dual-role access (Owner vs. Guest Viewer)**:
   - **Owner View**: The original creator accessing the tag sees both the
     archival facts and their private journal entries, with full write/edit
     capabilities.
   - **Guest / Viewer View**: Any other device tapping the tag sees only the
     curated artwork facts (title, artist, movement, Wikipedia extract) or a
     read-only exhibition presentation. Private personal notes remain completely
     hidden and inaccessible to guests.

## Phase 2 -- Indie Artist Bridge (Future Vision)

> Not in scope for the first prototype. Documented to inform infrastructure
> decisions.

- Independent artists see the consumer tool and want it for their original work.
- Instead of the tag containing a Wikipedia summary, the artist writes their own
  manifesto, adds behind-the-scenes sketches, and attaches the NFC tag before
  selling the piece.
- The app transitions from a private journal to a tool for artist authentication
  and storytelling.

---

## Phase 3 -- Studio Model (Future Vision)

> Not in scope for the first prototype. Documented to inform infrastructure
> decisions.

- Larger organizations (galleries, brands, fashion labels) adopt the
  infrastructure.
- The studio sells "Exhibition Design as a Service" -- procuring anti-metal NFC
  tags, integrating them into frames or sculptures, and designing bespoke,
  read-only versions of the app matched to the client's branding.
- The consumer app is read-write (fluid, editable, personal). The studio product
  is read-only (curated, polished, locked down).
- The studio retains selectivity -- rejecting clients who would use the tags for
  cheap advertising, ensuring the technology stays tied to intentional, lo-fi
  storytelling.

## Business Context (Documented, Not In Scope)

The journal explores three business models that could apply as the project grows:

- **Bottom-Up / Product-Led Growth (PLG)** -- The product sells itself through
  grassroots exposure. A single user adopts it, others see it organically, and
  adoption grows without a sales pitch. (Historical examples: Figma, Slack,
  Notion.)
- **Scratch Your Own Itch** -- Building a tool to solve a hyper-specific personal
  problem, which later becomes a product. (Historical examples: Basecamp,
  Teenage Engineering.)
- **Hybrid Software + Service** -- Maintaining a proprietary tech stack while
  selling expertise and curation on top of it, commanding premium pricing for
  time and artistic direction rather than competing on software subscriptions.
